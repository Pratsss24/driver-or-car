Splitting the spread

Within a race, the spread of car paces splits into a part between teams (0.732 s a lap) and a part within a team (0.531 s a lap, over 178 team-races). The within-team part is not the driver. It is the driver plus damage, a strategy call, a lap of traffic the detector missed, and the ordinary error in fitting a pace to forty laps.

The same teammate gap measured on odd rounds against even rounds correlates 0.590 over 12 pairings, a reliability of 0.565. Barely more than half of what separates one pairing from another comes back in the other half of the season. That fraction is what tells the persistent part from the day, and a car's pace in one race is itself only known to ±0.182 s a lap, which is taken out separately.

What this does not do

It does not rank drivers across teams, measure anything in qualifying, or separate a driver from the car's suitability to their style — a car built around one driver flatters that driver, and that appears here as driver skill. It uses one season, which is why the race-to-race term is as large as it is, and it has no tyre compound, because the source does not record one.
